Key findings

  • At current FY2024-25 prices, no SA2 breaches the 4% absolute hardship threshold — the absolute tier map shows Low/Moderate only.
  • The relative tier map tells the real story: Victor Harbor, Goolwa, Moonta, and Wallaroo are in the Critical tier — retirement towns, not impoverished communities.
  • Average water cost burden across all SA Water SA2s: 1.49% of household income.
  • The same $1,160 annual bill is 0.7% of income in Burnside and 2.4% in Elizabeth.

Key findings

  • The FY2025-26 increase (+4.7%) was approved by ESCoSA — 5 SA2s cross into a worse absolute tier as a direct result.
  • At +10%, 13 SA2s shift tier. The first to cross: Yorke Peninsula South, Wallaroo, Moonta — the same coastal retirement cluster.
  • At +20%, 28 SA2s shift. No major metropolitan Adelaide suburb shifts until +25%.
  • The simulation engine is parameterised — it recalculates tiered billing (not a flat multiplier) so tier crossings are precise, not estimated.

Hardship Priority

SA Water's payment assistance program (2,732 statewide customers, $2,590 average debt) is mapped against predicted suburb-level need. The gap between predicted need and program reach is the policy blind spot this project surfaces.

Key findings

  • Estimated hardship need is modelled from water cost burden ratio and SA2 population — it estimates the volume of households likely to be experiencing payment stress.
  • The suburbs with the highest estimated need are not all in the Adelaide metro — Port Pirie, Berri, Renmark, and Elizabeth rank highest outside the city.
  • SA Water's published hardship program figure (2,732 customers) cannot be cross-referenced to SA2 level — no suburb-level uptake data is publicly available. The gap cannot be confirmed, only estimated.
  • This is named estimated hardship need, not a "gap" — the weighting method estimates need, not actual under-service.

ML Explainability — SHAP

The ML model predicts stress tier from SEIFA socioeconomic indexes only — no income, no bill. SHAP decomposes each prediction into per-feature contributions. This confirms SEIFA as a leading indicator of water affordability stress, before any direct income data is available.

Global feature importance
Beeswarm — Critical tier SA2s

Key findings

  • IRSAD (Index of Relative Socioeconomic Advantage and Disadvantage) and IEO (Index of Education and Occupation) carry the strongest predictive signal for Critical tier classification.
  • The model achieves macro F1 ~0.60–0.69 — genuine signal, not a tautology. The first model (F1 = 1.000) was leaky: it included income and bill as features, which directly encode the tier label.
  • Using SEIFA proxies only means the model could be applied to any regulated utility in Australia where income data lags ABS collection cycles.
  • Best AutoML model: ExtraTrees (F1 = 0.685 via PyCaret).

V2 — Rainfall Trend Overlay new

The suburbs nearest the affordability threshold are also in the fastest-declining rainfall zones. Drought years push usage (and bills) up — but retirement-income households cannot adapt with greywater systems or efficient appliances. The regulatory risk compounds. 55 BOM stations, linear trend 2000–2025.

Rainfall trend per SA2 (mm/yr)
Top 20 steepest rainfall declines

Key findings

  • 13 double-stress SA2s: Critical water cost burden and declining annual rainfall (2000–2025).
  • Notable double-stress suburbs: Elizabeth, Victor Harbor, Lonsdale, Wallaroo, Moonta, Yorke Peninsula North/South, Berri, Renmark, Port Pirie, Goyder.
  • SA-wide mean rainfall trend: −2.8 mm/yr. Most of the state has been in long-term rainfall decline for 25 years.
  • The worst-hit BOM district: Happy Valley Reservoir station (−3.9 mm/yr) — the station nearest to Adelaide's southern suburbs, covering Elizabeth, Lonsdale, Victor Harbor, and Torrens Island.
  • This is not a supply scarcity story — SA Water draws from a centralised network (Murray + reservoirs + desalination). The risk is behavioural: declining rainfall → more garden and outdoor watering → higher usage → higher bills for households with no income growth.
